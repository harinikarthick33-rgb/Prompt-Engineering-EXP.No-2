EX-02-Cross-Platform-Prompting-Evaluating-Diverse-Techniques-in-AI-Powered-Text-Summarization

AIM:

To evaluate and compare the effectiveness of prompting techniques (zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought, role-based) across different AI platforms (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot) in a specific task: text summarization.

SCENARIO:

You are part of a content curation team for an educational platform that delivers quick summaries of research papers to undergraduate students. Your task is to summarize a 500-word technical article on "The Basics of Blockchain Technology" using multiple AI platforms and prompting strategies.

Your goal is to determine which combination of prompting technique + platform provides the best summary in terms of:

1.Accuracy

2.Coherence

3.Simplicity

4.Speed

5.User experience

PARAGRAPH BEFORE APPLYING PROMPTING TECHNIQUES:

Climate change has become one of the most serious environmental challenges facing the world today. It refers to long-term changes in global temperatures and weather patterns, largely caused by human activities such as burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial production. These activities release greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide and methane into the atmosphere, which trap heat and gradually warm the planet. As a result, the Earth’s climate system is being disrupted, leading to rising global temperatures, melting glaciers, and more frequent extreme weather events. Many regions around the world are already experiencing the consequences of climate change, including stronger storms, longer droughts, and unpredictable rainfall patterns. These changes not only affect the environment but also have serious impacts on agriculture, water supply, and human health. For example, farmers may struggle to grow crops due to shifting seasons and reduced water availability, which can threaten food security in many parts of the world. Coastal communities face the risk of rising sea levels that may flood homes, damage infrastructure, and force people to relocate. In addition, warmer temperatures can increase the spread of certain diseases and create conditions that worsen air pollution. Scientists and environmental organizations emphasize the importance of reducing greenhouse gas emissions to slow the rate of climate change. Governments around the world are working to develop policies that promote renewable energy sources such as solar, wind, and hydropower. These alternatives produce far fewer emissions than traditional fossil fuels and can help create a more sustainable energy system. Individuals also play an important role in addressing climate change through everyday choices such as conserving energy, reducing waste, using public transportation, and supporting environmentally responsible products. Education and awareness are essential in encouraging people to adopt more sustainable lifestyles and understand the long-term importance of protecting the planet. While solving climate change is a complex global challenge, cooperation between countries, businesses, and communities can lead to meaningful progress. By investing in clean technology, protecting forests, and developing sustainable policies, humanity can work toward reducing environmental damage and preserving the Earth for future generations.

Role-based summary (researcher):

From a research perspective, climate change represents a multifaceted global challenge rooted in anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions. Empirical evidence shows that fossil fuel combustion, deforestation, and industrial activity have significantly increased atmospheric concentrations of carbon dioxide and methane, thereby intensifying the greenhouse effect. This has led to observable phenomena such as rising mean global temperatures, accelerated glacial melt, sea-level rise, and heightened frequency of extreme weather events.

The consequences extend beyond environmental systems, impacting agriculture through altered growing seasons and water scarcity, public health via increased disease transmission and air pollution, and coastal infrastructure through flooding and displacement. Current mitigation strategies emphasize transitioning to renewable energy sources, enhancing carbon sequestration through forest conservation, and promoting sustainable consumption patterns.

Research underscores that effective climate action requires coordinated international policy frameworks, technological innovation, and widespread public engagement. The long-term objective is to stabilize global emissions, adapt to unavoidable impacts, and preserve ecological and human systems for future generations.

USING GEMINI

Zero-shot summary:

Climate change, driven by human activities like fossil fuel combustion and deforestation, is causing a global rise in temperatures and erratic weather patterns. These shifts threaten food security, coastal stability, and public health. Addressing this crisis requires a multi-faceted approach involving government-led renewable energy transitions, international cooperation, and individual lifestyle changes to reduce carbon footprints and preserve the planet for the future.

Few-shot summary:

The Cause: Greenhouse gas emissions from industrial and human activities are trapping heat and disrupting the Earth’s climate.

The Impact: Melting glaciers and rising sea levels pose immediate threats to agriculture and coastal infrastructure.

The Solution: A global shift toward renewable energy, combined with individual conservation efforts, is essential to mitigating long-term damage.

Chain-of-thought summary:

Identify the Catalyst: Human industrial activity increases the concentration of $CO_2$ and methane.

Explain the Process: These gases create a "blanket" effect, trapping solar radiation (the greenhouse effect).

Observe the Symptoms: The resulting heat leads to thermal expansion of oceans, melting ice, and altered atmospheric currents.

Evaluate the Risk: This disrupts human systems, specifically food production and urban safety in coastal areas.

Determine the Response: Mitigation requires transitioning from carbon-heavy energy to solar/wind and adopting sustainable resource management

Role-Based Summary (Researcher):

 The data indicates a clear correlation between anthropogenic emissions and the destabilization of global climate systems. Our primary concern lies in the feedback loops—such as melting permafrost and shifting agricultural zones—which threaten global caloric yields and infrastructure integrity. From a research standpoint, the transition to a low-carbon economy is no longer elective; it is a structural necessity. We must prioritize high-albedo urban planning, reforestation, and the scaling of $CO_2$ mitigation technologies to stabilize the mean global temperature.
